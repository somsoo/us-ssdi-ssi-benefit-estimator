How Disability Benefits are Evaluated

1

Work History Check

SSDI checks whether you earned at least 20 Social Security credits during the 10 years before disability.

2

SGA Wage Screen

Gross work earnings must remain below the federal Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) threshold ($1,620/mo).

3

Calculate Monthly Sum

SSDI is determined by indexed past wages, while SSI provides a needs-based monthly floor up to $943/mo.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the key difference between SSDI and SSI disability benefits?

SSDI is an earned insurance benefit funded by payroll FICA taxes based on your work history, while SSI is a needs-based program for disabled individuals with limited income and assets.

Can I work while receiving SSDI disability payments?

Yes, but your gross monthly earnings cannot exceed the Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA) limit of $1,620 for non-blind individuals (or $2,700 for blind individuals) unless in a Trial Work Period.

Can I receive both SSDI and SSI benefits at the same time?

Yes, this is known as concurrent benefits. If your monthly SSDI payment is less than the maximum SSI federal benefit rate plus $20, SSI can supplement the difference.
